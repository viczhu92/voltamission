What we design and supply

Two categories, with different compliance stories — worth keeping separate when you're scoping a project.

A. Electric heating jackets (powered)

  • Tank, vessel, and reactor heating jackets
  • Drum heaters and IBC tote heaters
  • Pipe and gas-line heating jackets (semiconductor / process gas delivery style)
  • Valve, pump, and instrument heating covers
  • Custom silicone, PTFE-faced, and cloth-jacketed heaters; thermostat / PID / RTD control options

B. Removable insulation blankets (non-electric)

  • Valve, flange, steam-trap, and pump insulation covers
  • Heat exchanger and vessel-head blankets
  • Reusable, hook-and-strap or lace-on closure; fiberglass, silica, or aerogel cores

Energy-savings angle: payback is typically under one heating season on steam systems, and may qualify for provincial energy-efficiency incentive programs.

Electrical compliance in Canada, handled.

Electric heating products installed in Canada require certification by an accredited body (CSA, cULus, cETL) or a per-unit Field Evaluation (SPE-1000) accepted by provincial authorities — the ESA in Ontario. Skipping this step is how imported heaters get red-tagged at commissioning.

Our process: product engineered to CSA-relevant construction requirements at the factory → field evaluation arranged on delivery → you receive a labeled, inspection-ready unit.

Removable (non-electric) insulation blankets require no electrical certification — the fastest path for steam and hot-water systems.

Honest scoping: we do not currently supply hazardous-location (Class I Div 1/2) certified heaters.

What is a removable insulation blanket and when should I use one instead of an electric jacket?

A removable insulation blanket is a non-electric, reusable cover (hook-and-strap or lace-on, with fiberglass, silica, or aerogel cores) that reduces heat loss from valves, flanges, steam traps, pumps, and vessel heads. It requires no electrical certification, making it the fastest path for steam and hot-water heat retention. Use an electric heating jacket instead when the equipment needs active heating, not just insulation.
